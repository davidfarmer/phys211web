    Problem 9.7.

    The idea of “diminishing returns” says that while the entropy does increase with increasing thermal energy, the slope is decreasing (see Figure 70). The Einstein solid multiplicity, like most materials, shows this behavior. Here is how to see it:
    1. For an Einstein solid with 10 oscillators and 5 energy units, calculate how much the entropy increases, i.e. \(\Delta S\text{,}\) if you add one more energy unit (you may leave your answer in terms of \(k_B\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Now consider an Einstein solid with 10 oscillators and 15 energy units, and calculate how much the entropy increases if you add one more energy unit.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Do your answers to (a) and (b) confirm the diminishing returns? Explain why.

    Problem 9.8.

    For two Einstein solids \(A\) and \(B\text{,}\) the entropy as a function of thermal energy is given by
    \begin{equation*} S_A= k_B\, 400\ln(E_A/300) \qquad S_B = k_B\, 100\ln(E_B/800) \end{equation*}
    where \(E_A\) and \(E_B\) are the thermal energies of systems \(A\) and \(B\text{.}\) If the two solids are brought to thermal equilibrium, what relation, if any, can be made between the final energies \(E_{A,f}\) and \(E_{B,f}\text{?}\)

    Problem 9.15.

    Entropy applies to more than just heat flow. We can use entropy and the second law of thermodynamics to discuss movement of air in a room.
    1. Consider a room with only 100 gas molecules. Theoretically, the gas molecules can move anywhere in the room. Calculate the probability that all 100 of the molecules will be found on one particular side of the room.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Now, consider a real room with a realistic amount of gas in it — let’s say that there are \(10^{26}\) gas molecules in the room. Calculate the probability that all of these gas molecules will be found in one particular side of the room. (Note: the probability is so small that your calculator or computer might simply give “0” for the answer.)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Is it reasonable to say that you will “never” find all the air in one side of the room?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Now, write a couple of sentences explaining why it is (from a probability perspective) that when a perfume bottle is opened, the scent of the perfume will spread throughout the room.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. After the perfume smell has spread throughout the room, would you expect all of the perfume molecules to go back into the bottle? Discuss this using the entropic form of the second law of thermodynamics.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Problem 9.16.

    The graphs in the figure below give plots of entropy \(S\) vs. \(E_\text{therm}\) for two different solids, A and B. Solid A starts with indicated energy \(E_A\) and entropy \(S_A\text{,}\) and Solid B starts with \(E_B\) and \(S_B\text{.}\) When Solid A has energy \(E_A\text{,}\) the slope of the entropy vs. energy curve is \(dS_A/dE= 0.2\Xunits{K\)^{-1}\(}\text{,}\) and when Solid B has energy \(E_B\text{,}\) the slope of the entropy vs. energy curve is \(dS_B/dE = 0.4\Xunits{K\)^{-1}\(}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The two solids are brought into thermal contact with each other so that energy can flow between them.
    1. Which way will the energy flow: from A to B, from B to A, or will no energy flow? Give qualitative reasoning to support your answer.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Now let’s get quantitative. Calculate the approximate entropy changes \(\Delta S_A\) and \(\Delta S_B\text{,}\) and \(\Delta S_\text{total}\) if \(3\,\text{J}\) of energy flow between the two solids in the direction that you chose in part (a).
      🔗
      🔗
    3. By what factor has the multiplicity for the total system increased from this energy transfer? In other words, calculate the ratio of multiplicities \(\Omega_\text{after} /\Omega_\text{before}\text{.}\) Note: The answer you get will be a ridiculously, mind-boggling, impossible-to-put-into-words-just-how-huge-it-really-is number that you will not be able to calculate — you’ll have to express it as \(e^\text{ something really big }\text{.}\) To give you and idea of just how large this number is, if you were to write it as a digit followed by a bunch of zeros, and if each digit were \(5\,\text{mm}\) wide, the number would fill up several light years.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Explain in your own words why heat flows in this system when the two solids are brought into contact. Don’t use the words “entropy” or “second law” but rather explain it based on probabilities.
      🔗
      🔗
