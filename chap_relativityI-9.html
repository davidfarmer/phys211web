    Problem 2.4.

    A spaceship moving at constant speed \(0.80\,\text{lt-s/s}\) travels between two planets A and B in \(1000\,\text{s}\text{,}\) as measured by synchronized clocks on the planets. Calculate the elapsed time according to a clock carried on board the spaceship.

    Problem 2.5.

    How fast does a particle have to travel relative to clocks A and B, which are at rest relative to each other, in order that its elapsed time as read on a clock moving with the particle is one-tenth the elapsed time measured on clocks A and B? Express your answer both in lt-s/s and in m/s.

    Problem 2.7.

    A crew of astronauts travels at a speed of \(0.60c\) from Earth to the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, a distance of \(4.0\,\text{lt-yr}\) (as determined by observers on Earth).
    1. Calculate how long the trip takes according to observers at rest relative to the Earth.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Calculate the time for the trip as measured by a clock on the spaceship.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Based on your answer to b), calculate the distance from Earth to Proxima Centauri as determined by the astronauts using the relation “distance” \(=\) “speed” × “time”, where distance, speed and time are all measured from the astronauts’ reference frame.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Calculate the Earth-Proxima Centauri distance from the astronauts’ reference frame, but this time use length contraction. You should end up with the same result as for c).
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Think about the results from parts c) and d). This should convince you that length contraction and time dilation are really the same thing (i.e., you can’t have one without the other).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

You throw the ball at a speed of \(10\,\text{m/s}\) straight to your friend, who catches the ball; the given speed and direction are determined in your reference frame. You may ignore any vertical motion of the ball.
    🔗
    1. How long does it take for the ball to reach your friend according to you?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. How long does it take for the ball to reach your friend according to an observer standing at rest on the ground? (This is a dumb question in classical physics — if the answer isn’t obvious, you’re thinking too hard!)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. How far does the ball travel according to the observer standing on the ground?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. What is the speed of the ball according to the observer standing on the ground?

    Problem 2.15.

    Photons on a Train
    🔗
    Note: This is a relativistic physics problem.
    🔗
    Alice is riding in a box car of a train that is traveling along the tracks at a speed \(v=0.6\,\text{lt-ns/ns}\text{.}\) She just happens to have a light-clock with her (just like the one illustrated in Figure 15 in Chapter 2). Alice aligns the clock so that the light is aimed horizontally directly across the train car, perpendicular to the motion of the train. Alice sends a light pulse across the train, and notices that the light returns to the detector \(2\,\text{ns}\) later. Alice’s friend Bob is standing at rest on the ground as Alice and her light-clock speed by. Bob measures the round-trip time for the pulse of light in Alice’s light clock to be \(\Delta t_{ Bob}\text{.}\)
    1. What is the speed of the light pulse according to Alice?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Determine the distance between the emitter/detector and the mirror in the light-clock as determined by Alice.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What is the speed of the light pulse according to Bob? (This is a dumb question in relativistic physics — if the answer isn’t obvious, you’re thinking too hard!)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Determine length of the path traversed by the light pulse according to Bob in terms of the unknown time \(\Delta t_{ Bob}\).
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the distance traveled by the light pulse according to Bob in terms of \(\Delta t_{ Bob}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Use your results from (d) and (e) to determine a numerical value for \(\Delta t_\text{Bob}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Now use (2.4.3) to calculate \(\Delta t_{ Bob}\text{.}\) This answer should agree with the answer you determined in the previous part.
      🔗
      🔗
