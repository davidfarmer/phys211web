Problem 0.27 Blow Darts and Superballs.

There is nothing that makes a day more complete than firing a blow dart at a small, defenseless superball. Before doing this, though, here is some relevant information: the blow dart has a mass of \(2.5\,\text{g}\) and the superballs have masses 8.5 \text{g} and \(25\,\text{g}\text{.}\)
  1. Now, put your larger superball (the animal ball) at the edge of a table and softly fire a blow dart directly at it (do it until the blow dart hits almost straight on). By “softly” we mean don’t blow as hard as you usually do. If you do this correctly, the dart should bounce straight back, and the large ball will move forward with only a very small speed.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Now, the main question: What changes do you have to make such that the blow dart will continue forward after a head-on collision with another object? Predict what you need to do, write down your prediction, and then test out your theory. (In making your prediction you may assume that the collision is elastic.)
