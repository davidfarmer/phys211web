Example 12.3.2.

How curved is space near the sun? That is, how different are \(\Delta s\) and \(\Delta r\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution: First, find the Schwarzschild radius from (12.3.2) with \(M=M_\text{ sun } = 1.99\times 10^{30}{kg}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation} R_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2} = \frac{2\times 6.67\times 10^{-11}\,\frac{\text{ N\(\cdot\)m\(^2\) } }{\text{ kg\(^2\) } }\times 1.99\times 10^{30}{kg}}{(3.00\times 10^8{m/s})^2} = 2950{m}\tag{12.3.3} \end{equation}
🔗
Then use the general relativity relation between \(\Delta s\) and \(\Delta r\text{,}\) (12.3.1), at the location \(r=R_\text{ sun } =6.96\times 10^{8}{m}\) to get
\begin{align*} \Delta s \amp = \biggl(1-\frac{2950{m}}{6.96\times 10^{8}{m}}\biggr)^{-1/2}\Delta r\\ \amp = 1.0000021\,\Delta r. \end{align*}
🔗
That’s not looking very curved, but it turns out to be enough to direct the motion of all the planets in their orbits about the sun!
