Example 8.1.1 Sketching $p$ -$V$ diagrams.

A gas in a cylinder starts at a temperature \(T_1 = 300\,\text{K}\text{,}\) volume \(V_1 = 3.0\,\text{L}\text{,}\) and pressure \(p_1 = 100\,\text{kPa}\text{.}\) It is expanded at constant pressure to twice its initial volume. It is then compressed isothermally back to the original volume, after which it is cooled down at constant volume to a temperature which is half of the initial temperature. Sketch a \(p\) -\(V\) diagram for the entire process \(1\rightarrow 2\rightarrow 3\rightarrow 4\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution:
🔗
The \(p\) -\(V\) diagram for the process is displayed in Figure 56. The illustrated constant-pressure expansion \((1 \rightarrow 2)\) also results in a temperature increase. Since \(pV = nRT\text{,}\) a doubling of the volume (to \(6.0\,\text{L}\) at constant-pressure results in a doubling of the temperature (to \(600\,\text{K}\)) as well. So, after the isothermal compression \((2 \rightarrow 3)\text{,}\) the gas is at twice its initial temperature and therefore twice the initial pressure; i.e., \(p_3 = 200\,\text{kPa}\text{.}\) (You could also easily determine the pressure \(p_3\) by noting that with \(n\) and \(T\) both constant, \(p_2V_2 = p_3V_3\text{.}\)) The final process \((3 \rightarrow 4)\) brings the gas to a temperature half of its initial value, and since the final volume is the same as the initial volume, the final pressure must by half of the initial pressure; i.e., \(p_4 = 50\,\text{kPa}\text{.}\)
