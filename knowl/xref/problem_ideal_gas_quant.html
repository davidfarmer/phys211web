Checkpoint 8.8.16.

In this problem you will make some simplifying assumptions and estimate the pressure of a gas of nitrogen molecules from a microscopic picture of the gas. Assume that the gas is in a \(10{cm} \times 10{cm}\times 10{cm}\) box at room temperature, \(T = 22^\circ{C}\text{.}\) The assumptions are:
  • All the molecules travel at the speed \(v_\text{ therm }\) derived in Example 8.2.1. This is not actually true — there is a spread in molecular speeds around the average — but \(v_\text{ therm }\) is a typical speed.
    🔗
    🔗
  • One third of the molecules in the gas travel in the \(\pm x\)-direction, one third travel in the \(\pm y\)-direction, and one third travel in the \(\pm z\)-direction. This is obviously not true, but this assumption will simplify the calculations.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
  1. Calculate the numerical value of the change in the momentum of a single nitrogen molecule traveling in the \(x\)-direction after it collides elastically with the right wall of the container. (If you need help determining the speed of the nitrogen molecules, see Example 8.2.1.)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Calculate the number of times this single nitrogen molecule collides with the right wall of the container in 1 second.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Calculate the total change in momentum of the molecule in 1 second due to collisions with the right wall of the container.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Calculate the average force on the right wall of the container due to collisions with the single molecule.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. At room temperature and atmospheric pressure, the number density, (i.e., the number of molecules per unit volume) of nitrogen molecules is \(2.49\times 10^{19}{molecules/cm\)^{3}\(}\text{.}\) Use the second of our simplifying assumptions and calculate the average force on the right wall of the container due to all of the molecules in the gas.
    🔗
    🔗
  6. Calculate the pressure that the gas exerts on the right wall of the container. Compare your answer to atmospheric pressure (\(1{atm} = 1.01\times 10^5{Pa}\)).
