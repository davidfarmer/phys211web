Example 5.3.4.

Figure 5.3.5 shows a particle of mass \(9{MeV/ c^2 }\) and speed \(0.8c\) striking a stationary particle of mass \(5{MeV/ c^2 }\text{,}\) producing a single particle of mass \(16{MeV/ c^2 }\text{.}\) You might not be too happy here with the final particle having a mass of \(16{MeV/ c^2 }\text{,}\) but hold on a little longer — we’ll explain this shortly. (A little preview — this might be a good time to take a pen and scribble (5.3.2) out of existence.) In the next chapter, we’ll learn more rigorously how to determine the correct attributes of the final particle. Here we just want to check the conservation laws. (a) Check the conservation of relativistic momentum and relativistic energy in the rest frame of the \(5{MeV/ c^2 }\) particle. (b) Check the conservation of relativistic momentum and relativistic energy in the rest frame of the \(16{MeV/ c^2 }\) particle.
🔗
Solution: (a) We will use the relativistic definitions and conservation laws given in Eqs. (5.3.4)–(5.3.7). Conservation of momentum gives
\begin{equation} \frac{9{MeV/ c^2 }\times 0.8c}{\sqrt{1 - 0.8^2}} + 0 = \frac{16{MeV/ c^2 }\times 0.6c}{\sqrt{1 - 0.6^2}},\tag{5.3.8} \end{equation}
and conservation of energy gives
\begin{equation} \frac{9{MeV/ c^2 }\times c^2}{\sqrt{1 - 0.8^2}} + \frac{5{MeV/ c^2 }\times c^2}{\sqrt{1 - 0^2}}= \frac{16{MeV/ c^2 }\times c^2}{\sqrt{1 - 0.6^2}}.\tag{5.3.9} \end{equation}
🔗
The momentum equation gives \(12{MeV/ c } = 12{MeV/ c }\text{,}\) while the energy equation gives \(15{MeV} + 5{MeV} = 20{MeV}\text{.}\) So the conservation laws are satisfied in this frame.
🔗
(b) Now, transform to a frame in which the final particle is at rest, by viewing from a spaceship moving at \(0.6c\) to the right. The velocity transformations give
\begin{align*} u_{16}^\prime =\mathstrut \amp \frac{u_{16}-v}{1 - u_{16}v/c^2} = \frac{0.6c -0.6 c}{1-0.6^2} = 0\\ u_{5}^\prime =\mathstrut \amp \frac{u_{5}-v}{1 - u_{5}v/c^2} = \frac{0 -0.6 c}{1-0\times 0.6} = -0.6c = -\frac{3}{5}c\\ u_{9}^\prime =\mathstrut \amp \frac{u_{9}-v}{1 - u_{9}v/c^2} = \frac{0.8c -0.6 c}{1-0.8\times 0.6} = \frac{5}{13}c \simeq 0.385c \end{align*}
🔗
In this new frame, the collision appears as in Figure 5.3.6. When we check the relativistic conservation laws in this new frame, we find:
\begin{equation} \frac{9{MeV/ c^2 }\times 5c/13}{\sqrt{1 - (5/13)^2}} - \frac{5{MeV/ c^2 }\times 3c/5}{\sqrt{1 - (3/5)^2}}= \frac{16{MeV/ c^2 }\times 0}{\sqrt{1 - 0^2}}\tag{5.3.10} \end{equation}
\begin{equation} \frac{9{MeV/ c^2 }\times c^2}{\sqrt{1 - (5/13)^2}} + \frac{5{MeV/ c^2 }\times c^2}{\sqrt{1 - (3/5)^2}}= \frac{16{MeV/ c^2 }\times c^2}{\sqrt{1 - 0^2}}\tag{5.3.11} \end{equation}
🔗
The momentum equation gives
\begin{equation} \frac{15}{4}{MeV/ c } -\frac{15}{4}{MeV/ c } = 0,\tag{5.3.12} \end{equation}
which checks out, while the energy equation gives
\begin{equation} \frac{39}{4}{MeV} +\frac{25}{4}{MeV} = 16{MeV}\tag{5.3.13} \end{equation}
which also checks out. This means the conservation laws are true in both the original and the new frame, and the Relativity Principle is upheld with Einstein’s new definitions.
