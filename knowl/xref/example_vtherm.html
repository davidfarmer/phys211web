Example 7.2.1 Speed of Nitrogen in the Atmosphere.

What is the typical speed of a nitrogen molecule in the atmosphere at room temperature of \(22^\circ\,\text{C}\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution: First we need to convert the temperature to Kelvin: \(T = 273 + 22 = 295\,\text{K}\text{.}\) The molar mass of elemental nitrogen is \(14\,\text{g/mol}\text{.}\) However, nitrogen in the air is in molecular form, \(N_2\text{,}\) which has two nitrogen atoms per molecule, and a molar mass of \(28\,\text{g/mol}\text{.}\) We may use (7.2.4), but to be consistent with SI units, we should convert the molar mass to kilograms:
\begin{align*} v_\text{therm} \amp = \sqrt{\frac{3 R T}{M}} = \sqrt{ \frac{3(8.31\,\text{ J/mol \cdot K } )(295\,\text{K} )}{0.028\,\text{kg/mol} }}\\ \amp = 512\,\text{m/s} \end{align*}
which is the same as about 1150 m.p.h. Room temperature molecules are fast!
