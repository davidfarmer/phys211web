Example 11.4.6. A Two-Brick Heat Engine.

A hot brick initially at temperature \(T_H=400{K}\) is used as the source for a heat engine. An equal sized cold brick made of the same material, initially at temperature \(T_C=300{K}\text{,}\) is used to dump the waste heat. As the heat flows, the two bricks come to thermal equilibrium. Assuming the heat engine was maximally efficient, what is the final temperature?
🔗
Solution: You might think the final temperature should just be \(350{K}\text{,}\) but that would be true only if we didn’t intercept any of the heat and convert it to work.
🔗
There is a lot we aren’t given. We don’t know the amounts or the composition of the bricks, but we know they are identical. The hotter brick is going to cool from \(400{K}\) down to some \(T_f\text{,}\) so its entropy change will be \(\Delta S_H = nC\ln(T_f/400)\text{,}\) which is negative since \(T_f/400\lt 1\text{.}\) The colder brick will warm from \(300{K}\) to \(T_f\text{,}\) so its entropy change will be \(\Delta S_C=nC\ln(T_f/300)\text{.}\) For maximum efficiency, then,
\begin{equation} \Delta S_\text{ total } = \Delta S_H+ \Delta S_C = nC\ln\left(\frac{T_f}{400}\right) + nC\ln\left(\frac{T_f}{300}\right) = 0.\tag{11.4.16} \end{equation}
🔗
The \(nC\) factors divide out, and we get
\begin{equation} \ln T_f - \ln 400 + \ln T_f - \ln 300 = 0\tag{11.4.17} \end{equation}
and so
\begin{align*} 2\ln T_f = \ln(400\cdot 300) \amp \Rightarrow\amp T_f^2=400\cdot 300\\ \amp \Rightarrow\amp T_f = 346.4{K}. \end{align*}
🔗
We see that the bricks end up slightly cooler than \(350{K}\text{,}\) which makes sense since we skimmed off some of the energy as it was flowing from hot to cold.
