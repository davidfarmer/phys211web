Example 4.3.1 Say goodbye to the classical expression for momentum.

(For this example, we use the natural units of MeV, MeV/\(c\text{,}\) MeV/\(c^2\) and \(c\text{.}\) More detail on these units appears section 4.8.) Figure 29 shows a particle of mass \(9\,\text{MeV} /c^2\) and speed \(0.8c\) striking a stationary particle of mass \(5 \,\text{MeV} /c^2\text{,}\) producing a single particle. (a) Calculate the mass and speed of the single particle after the collision. (b) Show that classical momentum is not conserved in the frame in which the final particle is at rest.
🔗
Solution: The classical laws Eqs. (4.3.1) and (4.3.2) would yield
\begin{align*} \text{(4.3.1)} \amp \Rightarrow\amp 9\,\text{MeV} /c^2\times 0.8c + 5\,\text{MeV} /c^2\times 0 = m_3u_3\\ \amp \Rightarrow\amp u_3 = \frac{7.2\,\text{MeV} /c} {14\,\text{MeV} /c^2} = 0.514c \end{align*}
and
\begin{align*} \text{(4.3.2)} \amp \Rightarrow\amp 9\,\text{MeV} /c^2 + 5\,\text{MeV} /c^2 = m_3\\ \amp \Rightarrow\amp m_3 = 14\,\text{MeV} /c^2. \end{align*}
🔗
Transform now to a frame in which the final particle is at rest. This clearly means that we should view the collision from a spaceship traveling with particle 3 at \(0.514c\) to the right, relative to the original observer. Equation (4.2.4) gives
\begin{align*} u_3^\prime =\mathstrut \amp \frac{u_3 - v}{1 - u_3v/c^2} = \frac{0.514c - 0.514c}{1-0.514^2} = 0\\ u_5^\prime =\mathstrut \amp \frac{u_5 - v}{1 - u_5v/c^2} = \frac{0-0.514c}{1-0\times 0.514} = -0.514c\\ u_9^\prime =\mathstrut \amp \frac{u_9 - v}{1 - u_9v/c^2} = \frac{0.8c - 0.514c}{1-0.8\times0.514} = 0.486c \end{align*}
🔗
In the new primed frame, the collision appears as in Figure 30. Checking the classical momentum conservation law in the new frame gives
\begin{equation} 9\,\text{MeV} /c^2\times 0.486c + 5\,\text{MeV} /c^2\times (-0.514c) = m_3 \times 0.\tag{4.3.3} \end{equation}
🔗
But the left side of this equation here works out to be \(1.80\,\text{MeV} /c\) which is NOT equal to the right side (which is 0). So, classical momentum is not conserved in this new frame. Therefore, either (a) conservation of momentum isn’t a valid law of physics; (b) the Relativity Principle (invariance of the laws of physics) is violated; or (c) we need a new definition for momentum.
