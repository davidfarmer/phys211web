Checkpoint 1.0.2. Graphing Motion II: Spring and Hopping Popper.

  1. Take your round metal spring and your “hopping popper” (the hemispherical plastic toy). Stretch the last few turns of the spring and jam the hopping popper in from the side (it looks a bit like a Pac-Man when you do this). When you have the hopping popper jammed into one end of the spring, hold the spring from the other end, and let the spring/hopping popper system hang vertically and allow it to come to rest (you are welcome to use your other hand to help the end of the spring with the hopping popper stop moving).
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Call the position of the hopping popper when it is motionless \(y = 0\text{.}\) Now, pull the hopping popper end of the spring straight down approximately 6 — 10 inches (the exact distance isn’t that important) and release the hopping popper end of the spring. Make sure that the subsequent motion of the hopping popper and the spring is as vertical as possible.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Make qualitative plots of the hopping popper’s (i) vertical position versus time for a few cycles, and (ii) vertical velocity versus time. Use your sketch of the hopping popper’s vertical velocity versus time to make a qualitative sketch of the hopping popper’s vertical acceleration vs. time. (Hint: ask the same questions as in Problem A 1.0.1 if you are stuck. Specifically consider the hopping popper’s position and velocity when it is at the maximum distance from \(y = 0\) and also consider the hopping popper’s position and velocity when it is passing through \(y = 0\text{.}\))
