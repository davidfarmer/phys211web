    1. Mission control on Earth sends an encoded message (a flashing beacon) to Duck Dodgers warning him about the progress of the Martian ship. The light pulses travel at a speed \(c\) relative to observers on the Earth. How fast are the pulses traveling relative to Duck Dodgers?

      🔗
    2. Duck Dodgers doesn’t understand the message that he received, so he sends a radio message back toward the Earth asking for clarification. The radio signal is traveling at a speed \(c\) relative to the Duck. How fast is the signal traveling relative to observers on the Earth?

      🔗
    3. The radio message is intercepted by the Martian who is behind Duck Dodgers but traveling in the same direction at a speed \(0.8c\) relative to the Earth. How fast is the radio message going relative to the Martian?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. The radio message is also intercepted by one of the Martian’s monsters who is traveling back toward the Earth to attack. The monster is traveling at a speed \(0.9c\) relative to the Martian. How fast is the radio signal relative to the monster?

    Checkpoint 5.10.20.

    Are momentum and energy conserved?
    🔗
    A mad scientist at rest in a lab on the earth is colliding particles together to make more massive particles. She creates head-on collisions of particles with a mass of \(4{GeV/ c^2 }\) and speed \(0.6c\) to create particles at rest with a mass of \(10{GeV/ c^2 }\) (see Figure 5.10.20.)
    🔗
    In the rest frame of the scientist, the \(4{GeV/ c^2 }\) particles have equal and oppositely directed momenta, and the \(10{GeV/ c^2 }\) particle is at rest, so momentum is conserved.
    🔗
    An observer in a rocket flying over the lab at a speed \(v=0.6c\) views this experiment (see Figure 5.10.20.)
    1. Test whether relativistic momentum is conserved according to the observer in the rocket. Your test should include detailed numerical calculations demonstrating the conservation (or non-conservation) of relativistic momentum.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Test whether relativistic energy is conserved according to the scientist in her lab. Your test should include detailed numerical calculations demonstrating the conservation (or non-conservation) of relativistic energy.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Test whether relativistic energy is conserved according to the observer in the rocket. Your test should include detailed numerical calculations demonstrating the conservation (or non-conservation) of relativistic energy.
